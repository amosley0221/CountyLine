DESIGNER'S NOTE TO PRODUCER — assumptions, context, reasoning
Context used. Your brief + six reference frames (wall map, aerial of the seat, hotel kitchen/basement, Tanner Camp A, jail office at night, courthouse square at noon). The references set palette and light: caliche dust, brick, filament, dirty overcast. I took them as canon for materials; where a frame contradicts the brief (see 03, "Map discrepancies") the brief wins and the frame goes to the gazetteer as a note.
Assumptions. 1080p is the floor: nothing in HUD under 22px, journal body 20px at 1080p. UI is paper-diegetic: every screen is an object Reed could hold (county book, carbon report, front page, docket stub). No holographic panels, no vignette blur behind menus — the world keeps rendering, dimmed like a lamp turned down. Rebel path reuses every screen with the county chrome stripped (no star, no brass, boarding-house letterhead); Reed keeps his own compass and Book.
Reasoning. The four ledgers are never bars. They are columns of handwritten entries with a running total in the margin — you read the trend the way a clerk would. Headlines borrow the 1927 weekly (Old Standard); body is a carbon copy (Courier Prime); Reed's pencil is Caveat, used only for things he wrote by hand. Tokens are named for materials so Unreal material instances and UMG styles share vocabulary.
ONE-SHEET · PC · UNREAL ENGINE 5 · THIRD-PERSON
The County Line
A narrative open-world lawman sim.
Rivas County, West Texas. Autumn 1926 – November 1928.
The job

You are Acting Sheriff Reed, appointed after Hal Voss resigned for his health. There is a body in an irrigation ditch east of town and a report on your desk that already says drunk, drowned, closed. You can sign it. You can also not.

The county is 38 miles by 26. A courthouse, a jail, a hotel with two economies, an oil lease with two camps, a river with one legal ford and one that isn't on the clerk's map. You keep the star by keeping the commissioners and the lease money comfortable. You lose it the same way. The map stays open either way.

"It's Reed. I'm at the mile marker by the Tanner lease. If the wrecker's coming, tell them to bring a chain."
— tone reference for every line of UI copy
Pillars
  1. Jurisdiction is the gameplay. Whose land, whose worker, whose vote.
  2. The star is a job. Keep it, climb it, or lose it. The county doesn't close when you do.
  3. Combat is rare, ugly, politically expensive. Revolver, Winchester, shotgun. Every gunfight writes a Courthouse line.
  4. Paper is a weapon. Reports, ledgers, warrants, columns become evidence later.
  5. Rooms over roads. Courthouse, jail desk, hotel kitchen, lease office, depot, porches.
Two paths, one county
Badge. Commissioners, budget, deputies, election '28. County Ford. Jail desk.
Fired. Same county. Ford revoked, civilian coupe, boarding-house phone. Lease security or River Road families.
Four ledgers (journal only, never meters)
CourthouseJudge Helm, clerk, commissioners. Promotion needs it high. StreetCamps, River Road, porches. Rebel viability lives here. CapitalTanner Lease, hotel, mercantile credit. Decides if you're cheaper gone. HomeMrs. Lang's, church, the doctor. What's left when the star is.
Not this
  • No duster silhouette, no sunset walkdown, no trough standoff.
  • No black-and-white cruiser, no bubble light.
  • No honor bar. No minimap on the coat.
  • No horror, no supernatural, no comedy-western.
Protagonist: Sam Reed, he/him, mid-40s. "Sheriff" on duty, "Mr. Reed" after. Camera: over-the-shoulder follow, protagonist always visible. Single player, 20–30 hr campaign. Travel: Court→Camps 40–50 min dry, 2–4 hrs after rain. Fast travel: depot tickets or roads already driven.

STAMPS · the county's marks
CLOSED DENIED RECEIVED · CLERK SERVICES TERMINATED
Rotate −6° to +4°, 85% opacity, slight ink starve on one edge. Stamps are applied by others; Reed's own marks are pencil.
BUTTONS · menu only
SIGN HOLD FOR INQUIRY DEPUTIZE (no budget)
Outline = available. Filled ink = focused. Dashed = blocked, with the reason in parentheses. No hover glow; focus is a pencil underline appearing left-to-right, 90ms.
Do / Don't
DO
  • Light UI like the room: filament warm at night, flat grey under overcast, hard white at noon.
  • Let paper carry information. If a stat matters, it's a line in a book someone keeps.
  • Write copy tired and specific: mile markers, names, times, who to call.
  • Show the campaign hat and wool/khaki coat in every key-art silhouette.
  • Use one derrick on the western horizon as the county's clock — more appear only as Act II progresses.
  • Default light: noon hard, dirty overcast, green-white electric night.
DON'T
  • No honey-sunset grade as default. Golden hour is an event, not a look.
  • No meters, bars, karma dials, or numeric reputation anywhere in-world.
  • No minimap. No objective markers floating over people.
  • No score on the square. No wanted level. No dead-eye, no weapon wheel.
  • No western ornament: no rope borders, no wanted-poster type, no bullet holes.
  • No game-UI blue, no glow except a lit filament, no glass or holographic panels.
  • No mythic copy. If a line would fit on a movie poster, cut it.
